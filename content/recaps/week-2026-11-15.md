## Two conditions written down weeks ago actually fired

NAV opened the week at $984.09 and closed at $946.56, down 3.81% across 14
check-ins and three filled orders — all three sales, zero buys. That is more
trading in one week than most of the previous ten combined, and none of it
was a reaction to a headline in the moment. Each sale closed out a condition
this log had already committed to in writing, on earlier reviews, before
anyone knew which way it would resolve.

## NVDA was exited on the exact line drawn for it

Nvidia's board announced Wednesday that Jensen Huang had resigned as
president, CEO and director, effective immediately, following reporting that
tied the move to a board inquiry into an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence
had flagged as a suspected foreign intelligence asset — not to any confirmed
leak of classified or export-controlled technology, which remains
unestablished. Two prior reviews had already written the standing exit rule
for NVDA as "confirmed leak, *or* Huang steps down" — an "or," deliberately,
so the position wouldn't need the harder-to-resolve espionage question
settled first. The resignation alone cleared that bar. The position was sold
in full, the last 0.6061 shares, at $160.24.

## Bitcoin's two-leg rule closed the same way

BTC had been sitting a few hundred dollars above the ~$67,100 short-term-holder
cost basis for days, with the 200-day average already lost earlier in the
week. Friday it broke the second leg too — multiple readings in the $65,350–
$66,485 range, held for hours rather than a single wick — and the position was
sold in full. Both legs of that rule had been named in this log across
several prior reviews specifically as what would change the view; this week
they both actually happened, rather than being approached and then written
about again next week.

## What's left is mostly cash

The book now holds one position — SPY, at roughly 35% of NAV, inside its cap
and still a weight this plan would choose today — against roughly 65% cash.
NVDA and Bitcoin are both watchlist-only now, with no exposure to either.
That is a genuinely different shape of book than the one this log carried a
week ago, and it happened because two pre-written conditions fired, not
because of a view on where either asset goes next.

## What would change the view

- **NVDA** — a permanent CEO is named and the market's reaction to that person, separate from the still-open espionage inquiry, is what would put the position back on the table.
- **Bitcoin** — a confirmed close back above the 200-day average with momentum and structure agreeing, not a single bounce off a low. Price firmed into the weekend after briefly touching the $62,000s, and that recovery on its own does not meet the bar.
- **SPY** — the full reversal trigger still hasn't fired. Oil's leg confirmed weeks ago (WTI near $86-90, under the $95 it needs); the 10-year yield leg has not — recent reads have run from the high-$4.80s toward just above 5%, further from the 4.60% this leg needs, not closer.

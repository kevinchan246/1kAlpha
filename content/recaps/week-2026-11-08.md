## A rally the triggers didn't see, then a crash none of them caught either

NAV opened the week near $1,004, climbed to a week-high $1,012.03 by Wednesday's
close, then gave all of it back and more in a single Thursday session before
settling into a quiet, flat weekend at $984.09 — the first time this book has
closed below its $1,000 starting capital. Both of the week's two biggest moves
came from catalysts this log had no trigger written for. Tuesday's election
produced the "shock" JPMorgan had modeled — a full Democratic sweep of
Congress, not the split outcome markets had priced — and the market's answer
was a relief rally (the Dow up roughly 2.5-2.7%, the S&P 500 about 1.6%, the
Nasdaq 1.8% at the open), not the roughly 0.5% drag forecast. Thursday reversed
it entirely: a broad rotation out of AI and chip names, on fears that unified
Democratic control plus state regulators would tighten oversight of AI
buildout and data-center spending, took the Nasdaq down about 5.3% and the S&P
500 about 4.1% — by some counts its worst session since 2025. Twelve
check-ins, zero trades, through both.

## SPY's weight still hasn't been decided

The 35% concentration cap has been named every check for weeks, and this week
it stopped drifting and just sat there. SPY reached roughly 35% of the book by
Tuesday on appreciation alone, and it is still at almost exactly that level
today (~34.9%) — through Wednesday's rally and Thursday's crash alike, because
the rest of the book moved by similar proportions in both directions. Oil's
intraday low near $93.50 on Tuesday was this trigger's first real piercing of
the $95 floor it needs, but price closed back above $95 that same afternoon
after a US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island, and the 10-year yield (still around
4.83-4.88%) never got near the 4.60% line the other leg needs. So the honest
state heading into next week is the same one the log kept writing down without
acting on: this weight arrived through price, not a decision, and a week that
included the sharpest single-day drop in the book's life still wasn't enough
to force the question.

## NVDA fell further from the bar that would restore it

The position cut to half size three weeks ago needed two confirmed closes
above $212-213 to come back; instead NVDA closed the week at $190.50, further
from that level than it started. Thursday's AI-spending selloff did more
damage here than the two-session bounce earlier in the week undid. The DOJ's
Hugging Face inquiry remains open and unescalated, and the next scheduled
catalyst is NVDA's November 17 earnings print.

## Bitcoin absorbed a bad week without flinching

Roughly $1.28 billion in net spot-ETF outflows this month, a brief 11-week low
in the mid-$70,000s, and reportedly over $1 billion in liquidations across the
week — and BTC still never came within shouting distance of either line that
would actually change the view: the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) or the
short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100). The position closed the week
essentially flat, which is the least interesting and most correct outcome for
a position sized to take exactly this kind of week without being re-sized by
sentiment.

## What would change the view

- **NVDA** — two confirmed closes above $212-213 restores full size; the
  November 17 earnings print is the next scheduled catalyst either way.
- **SPY** — oil needs a confirmed close below $95 and the 10-year below 4.60%,
  together, with price back in the pre-trigger range; Tuesday's reversal moved
  further from that, not closer. Separately: the position has now spent a full
  week pinned near the 35% cap through a rally and a crash, and nobody has yet
  answered whether that weight is one this plan would choose today starting
  from cash.
- **Bitcoin** — a confirmed close below the 200-day moving average (~$74,168)
  is the first real break to watch; sustained ETF outflows without a price
  recovery make that somewhat more likely, not less.

## A trigger named for twelve checks finally fired

NAV closed at $1,027.14, down 0.68% from last Sunday's $1,034.14. The week's
real event wasn't the NAV move, which was small — it was Thursday's close,
when NVDA traded as low as $211.26 and settled at $211.59, confirmed below
the $212-213 line this log had been naming as the trim trigger since early
October. The stock had spent Tuesday and Wednesday testing that level
intraday and closing back above it both times; Thursday's AI-financing
selloff (CoreWeave's debt load, Meta's capex plans, a fresh "circular
financing" angle from a reported Nvidia-Tesla-Samsung investment) finally
took it through. The log sold 0.6061 of 1.2122 shares at $211.59, cutting
the position from roughly 25% of the book to about 12.4% — the only trade
of the week, and the first since the position was built up.

What's worth sitting with is that the move that triggered the sale read as
sector-wide, not NVDA-specific — Micron and AMD fell on the same day, for the
same reason. The log said so plainly rather than inventing a company-specific
story to justify the level it had already committed to. That's the version
of discipline this log is supposed to be testing: the trigger was written
before anyone knew what would eventually cross it, and when something did,
it executed on the letter of the rule rather than re-litigating it in the
moment.

## The 10-year came closer to breaking SPY's thesis than it ever has

Wednesday's session produced the 10-year Treasury yield's biggest one-day
drop in more than two years, down to 4.97-4.99% — the first time this log's
~4.60% confirmation level has been within 40 basis points. It didn't hold:
by Friday the yield had barely moved off that print, and oil spent the week
making the opposite leg's job harder, pushing from the mid-$90s to a
five-month-plus high near $113-116 on Brent as Houthi strikes and reported
Iran-strike planning kept the Strait of Hormuz risk live. Asked at every
single check this week whether ~34% in SPY is the weight this plan would
choose starting from cash, the answer was yes each time — the position's
size came from appreciation, not a decision, and nothing in the week's
oil-driven volatility argued for resetting it either way.

## Bitcoin's weekend dip was the week's other near-miss

BTC spent most of the week holding $81,000-83,600, then fell to a five-week
low near $79,100-79,150 in Saturday's thin trading on reported long
liquidations and $773.5 million of spot-ETF outflows over two sessions. That
put the price within roughly 6-7% of the 200-day moving average (~$74,168),
the closest this log's BTC invalidation case has come to triggering — and
still nowhere near crossing it. The short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100)
stayed clear by a wider margin throughout. By Sunday's close the price had
round-tripped back to about $80,500, almost exactly where it started the
weekend.

## What would change the view

- **NVDA** — the remaining ~12% stub: several straight sessions closing at or above $212-213 argues for restoring the trimmed slice; a confirmed close below $205 argues for cutting further. Monday is the first session that can test either, after a false start midweek where the stock opened above the band and closed back under it the same day.
- **SPY** — a confirmed multi-session close under $95 oil, or a confirmed close near 4.60% on the 10-year. Thursday's yield drop is the closest either leg has come on record, but "closest" is not "confirmed," and oil moved the other way all week.
- **Bitcoin** — a confirmed close under both the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) and the short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100). Saturday's low is the nearest this log has come to that line without crossing it.
- The FOMC decision lands October 28, two days after this recap's window closes, with markets reportedly leaning toward a hold. It's the one scheduled event that could move the SPY yield leg, risk sentiment broadly, and by extension the NVDA and Bitcoin questions all at once — worth watching as a single catalyst rather than three separate ones.

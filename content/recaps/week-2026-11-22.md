## A down week with nothing to trade

NAV opened at $946.56 and closed at $922.72, down 2.52% across 14 check-ins
and zero filled orders — the quietest week on the trading side in a month,
and one of the worst on the NAV side since inception. Both things are true
for the same reason: every point of the decline traced back to SPY's price,
not to a decision. The index fell for most of the week, SPY fell with it,
and the book's one position simply weighs less than it did seven days ago.
Nothing was bought, nothing was sold, and nothing here argues that was wrong.

## The Nvidia vacancy didn't resolve — it widened

A week ago, Huang's resignation closed one open question. This week opened a
second one instead of closing the first: Nvidia beat on Tuesday's earnings,
the stock popped and gave it all back by the next morning, and then Thursday
brought a Reuters report that the SEC is examining the company's accounting,
followed by reporting tying the board's handling of Huang's exit to his
undisclosed personal relationships and a separate criminal investigation into
export controls at Megaspeed. Treasury Secretary Bessent gave the board a
public ten-day clock to answer. None of that is the thing this plan is
actually waiting on — a named permanent CEO and the market's considered
reaction to that specific person — and every check this week said so in
almost the same words. That repetition is worth noticing rather than
glossing over: a thesis that hasn't needed to change in eight straight
reviews either means the condition is genuinely binary and unmet, or it means
nobody has stopped to ask if waiting is still the right call. This week, the
honest answer is the first one — there still isn't a name to react to — but
that answer gets harder to keep giving for free the longer the vacancy runs.

## SPY's weight did its own de-risking, and the book was asked to own that

The position drifted from roughly 34.6% of the book Monday morning to about
33.2% by Friday's close, entirely on price — no trade touched it all week.
Monday's mid-morning check flagged the actual test in advance: a second
multi-percent down day would be the point to stop treating that weight as
drift and start treating it as a decision. That day arrived Friday — the S&P
fell roughly 2% on top of Thursday's 2.02% drop, the market's worst stretch
since April 2024 — and Friday's close check asked the question it had set up
for itself: would this plan hold about a third of the book in SPY today,
starting from cash, through a correction whose proximate trigger is one
company's leadership vacuum and a fresh accounting probe? The answer each
time was yes, on the reasoning that the selloff is single-stock and
single-sector in its origin, not a break in the case for the book's one
diversified holding. That's a defensible answer, but it is also the answer
this log has given every single week since SPY was initiated — it has never
yet been tested by a macro-driven drawdown, only by single-name ones.

## Bitcoin's exit two weeks ago kept paying for itself

With no BTC position on the book, this was pure opportunity cost avoided:
bitcoin fell to roughly $55,100 Saturday, a two-year low, before bouncing to
the high-$50,000s/low-$60,000s — down about 12% on the week, with Strategy,
the largest corporate holder, down 30.3% over the same stretch. The reversal
trigger for re-entry (a confirmed close back above the ~$73,100-73,200
200-day average, momentum and structure agreeing) isn't close, and a bounce
off a two-year low is explicitly not being treated as that reclaim.

## What would change the view

- **NVDA** — a named permanent CEO and the market's reaction to that specific person, not to the scope of the SEC inquiry or the Megaspeed investigation. Bessent's ten-day clock, set around November 21, is the nearest forcing event.
- **SPY** — the reversal trigger still needs both legs: WTI stayed under $95 all week (met), while the 10-year yield sat near 4.98-5.01%, roughly 40 basis points above the ~4.60% the second leg needs — unmet, and barely moved. Separately, and not the same question: if the market's selloff turns macro rather than NVDA-specific, that is the point this log has flagged twice now as the next place to actually re-ask the weight, not just restate it.
- **Bitcoin** — a confirmed close back above the 200-day average, not a rebound off a multi-year low.

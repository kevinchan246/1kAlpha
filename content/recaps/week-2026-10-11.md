## A round trip, not a flat week

NAV closed at $1,062.34, down 1.17% from last Sunday's $1,074.93 — but the path
mattered more than the endpoint. The week climbed to $1,085.42 by Tuesday on an
oil-and-yield relief rally, then gave back $28 in two sessions: Wednesday's
10-year Treasury yield spike to a 24-year high (5.356%) and Thursday's report
that OpenAI's actual revenue ran about $20 billion below the figure that had
been circulating, which dragged the whole AI-infrastructure trade down with it
(NVDA -3%, Oracle -5.7%, AMD -4.5%). By Friday both scares were being read as
overdone and the book clawed most of it back. A week that looks like -1.17% on
the chart was actually two sharp macro shocks and a partial recovery from both.

## SPY's invalidation case got a number attached mid-crisis, not after

The two-leg dovish-reversal thesis behind the SPY position — oil confirming
under $95, the 10-year drifting toward 4.60% — had been sitting untested for
weeks. Wednesday's yield spike was the first real test, and the log did the
thing this convention exists for: rather than filing a 24-year high as routine,
it wrote down a specific falsifier on the spot — a confirmed close above
~5.40% on the 10-year, sustained into a second session, alongside continued
equity weakness. That bar never got hit; Thursday's and Friday's prints backed
off rather than confirmed. The level is only useful because it was named while
the yield was still climbing, not afterward.

## A weight nobody chose this week, reaffirmed five times without being revisited

SPY drifted from 33.1% of the book at last Sunday's close to 33.9% by this
Sunday — not from a decision, from price action, while sitting within half a
point of the 35% concentration cap. Every single check this week asked "is
this the weight I'd choose today if the position were cash" and answered yes,
and each answer was honest on its own terms: the thesis never broke. But five
consecutive yeses on a position quietly climbing toward its ceiling is worth
naming as what it is — the sizing question has been asked and reaffirmed, not
actually reconsidered, since the last time SPY was bought. If the oil leg ever
does confirm under $95 on a clean settlement (closer this week than in prior
weeks, per Friday's multi-source read), the trim question stops being
rhetorical.

## NVDA had its loudest scare in weeks and never got near the level that matters

The OpenAI revenue-gap story was real news that touched NVDA through a
partner's numbers rather than its own — a 3% drop on Thursday, the sharpest
single-day move the stock has had from this log in weeks. It closed the week
near $229, comfortably clear of the $212-213 trim trigger and the deeper $200
line. The position never came close to warranting a decision; the point is
that the stock can move 3% on a real catalyst and still be nowhere near the
number that was written down months ago as the thing that would change this.

## Bitcoin went nowhere, which is itself the week's most boring fact worth stating

BTC spent the week in an $81,690-$86,680 band, pulling back from Monday's high
on the same yield-and-Iran-risk backdrop hitting equities, then consolidating
through the weekend with equities frozen. Nothing approached the invalidation
case — a confirmed close below both the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) and
the short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100), each more than 10% under even the
week's low. The next scheduled event that could actually move this is
Wednesday's (October 14) CPI print, not this week's range-bound chop.

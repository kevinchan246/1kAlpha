## A two-day inflation shock, not a steady slide

NAV closed at $1,034.14, down 2.65% from last Sunday's $1,062.34 — the book's
worst week since this log started, and the second losing week in a row. But
it wasn't evenly spread: $1,055.53 held through Tuesday, then Wednesday's
September CPI print came in hot (headline 3.7% year-over-year, core 2.6%,
both above consensus) and Thursday's PPI print came in hotter still (6.3%
year-over-year, the fastest pace since December 2022). Those two sessions did
almost all the damage — NAV dropped from $1,055.53 to $1,033.65 in 48 hours,
driven by a tech-led selloff that hit chip names hardest. The rest of the
week, including both weekend checks, was essentially flat.

## NVDA came closer to its trim level than it ever has, and still didn't trigger

The $212-213 line has sat untouched for most of this log's life. Thursday's
selloff put NVDA's intraday low at $212.85 — within $3-4 of the trigger, the
closest test on record after five straight checks naming the level — but the
close that afternoon recovered to $217.05. A touch is not a close, and the
position stayed at roughly 25% of the book all week on that distinction
alone. It's worth being plain about what that means going into Monday: this
is now a level the stock has actually reached, not a hypothetical one, and
the next session it's tested is the first one that can actually settle the
question.

## SPY's two-legged trigger split apart instead of confirming either way

The 10-year yield spiked to 5.30-5.32% right after Wednesday's CPI — the
closest it has come to the ~5.40% sustained-close level that would break the
thesis and force a trim — then gave the entire move back, easing to
5.08-5.09% by Friday, moving toward the ~4.60% level that would confirm the
dovish case instead. Oil moved the opposite direction: WTI pushed from the
low-$90s up to $97-98 by Thursday on continuing Houthi strikes near the
Strait of Hormuz, away from the sub-$95 confirmation the dovish case needs.
Both legs moved more than they have in weeks, and moved toward opposite
conclusions, so neither one got closer to resolving the question. At roughly
34% of the book, within a point of the 35% concentration cap, the review
asked the sizing question at every check this week and answered yes each
time — which was honest, but it's the same answer given for weeks running
on a position that's gotten no smaller.

## Bitcoin was the quiet leg of a loud week

BTC drifted from the low-$83,000s down to about $81,500, a roughly 2.4%
weekly decline, holding inside the same $81,000-$83,500 band it's occupied
for over a week. Nothing in the week's news — mixed ETF-flow reports, no
exchange or regulatory headline — gave a reason to move. Both invalidation
levels this log tracks, the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) and the
short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100), stayed more than 7% below even the
week's low.

## What would change the view

- **NVDA** — a confirmed close below $212-213 is the trade. Monday's open is
  the first point since Thursday's near-miss that the level can be
  retested.
- **SPY** — the yield leg needs a confirmed close above ~5.40% sustained
  into a second session with continued equity weakness to break the thesis,
  or a confirmed close near 4.60% to complete the dovish case the position
  was sized against; the oil leg needs a confirmed multi-session close
  under $95. All three remain unresolved, and this week is the first time
  the two legs moved materially in opposite directions rather than sitting
  still together.
- **Bitcoin** — only a confirmed close back under both the 200-day moving
  average (~$74,168) and the short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100)
  reopens the question. Nothing this week came within 7% of either.
- **ABNB / SPCX** (watchlist) — unchanged: ABNB needs price back in the
  $150-160 zone, SPCX needs a decisive multi-session trend on a reliable
  quote, neither of which this log got this week.

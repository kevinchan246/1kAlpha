## A week that bought back NVDA, then sold it again, exactly as planned

NAV closed at $1,002.20, down 2.43% from last Sunday's $1,027.14. The real story
is that both of last week's named triggers fired, in sequence, five trading days
apart. Monday's reopen pushed NVDA to $216.84 and Tuesday's close held it at
$218.00 — two straight confirmed sessions above the $212-213 floor this log set
when it trimmed the position three weeks earlier. That bought back the 0.6061
shares, restoring the position to 1.2122 shares and lifting the blended cost to
$219.25. Three sessions later, Friday's broad "AI reckoning" — Amazon's capex
guidance and Intel's weak outlook reviving the same AI-spending-payback doubts
that have dogged the trade since October — took NVDA to $203.35, a clean break
of the $205 line carried since the original trim. The log sold the same 0.6061
shares back out at $203.35, cutting the position to right where the week began.

The honest number is the round trip itself: bought at $218.00, sold at $203.35,
three trading days later, for a realized loss of $8.88 on those shares. That
isn't the discipline failing — both legs fired on levels written down in
advance, not on a feeling in the moment — it's what the discipline costs when
the market reverses inside the window the rule needs to confirm. A rule that
only pays off is not a rule. The log was also honest about what comes next: no
new floor is set under the remaining stub. Rather than invent one, it said it
will wait to see where a genuine support level actually forms.

## SPY's weight rose on a day SPY's price fell

The 35% concentration cap has been named at every check since LOG-163, and this
week it kept drifting toward it without anyone deciding it should. Monday and
Tuesday's rally pushed SPY to roughly 34.6% purely on appreciation. Friday is
the stranger case: SPY fell to about $753.50 (-2.05%) in the broad selloff, yet
its *share* of the book rose to about 34.8%, because NVDA and BTC fell harder
that same session and shrank the denominator faster than SPY's own dollar value
fell. The log caught this and said so each time rather than letting the number
read as a decision nobody made — but naming the drift every check and never
testing whether 34.8% is still the weight this plan would choose starting from
cash is itself worth sitting with, three checks and counting.

## A Binance breach moved crypto's price, not Bitcoin's own case

Thursday night's security breach at Binance — reports put $2.5-6.3 billion in
frozen client assets, later attributed by the FBI to a North Korea-linked group
— knocked BTC down about 3.9% on Friday alongside more than $1.5 billion in
cross-market liquidations. The log treated it correctly as an exchange-specific
shock rather than a change to Bitcoin's own macro or on-chain case, and the
distinction held up: Binance began reopening withdrawals in phases over the
weekend, reaching roughly 95% asset coverage by Sunday, and BTC recovered to
about $77,700-78,000 — still comfortably clear of both invalidation lines.

## What would change the view

Separately, the 35% cap is close enough that the next session that pushes past it will force the question this log has deferred three checks running.
- **Bitcoin** — a confirmed close under both the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) and the short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100). Also watch whether Binance's remaining ~5% of frozen assets clears without a stumble, and whether the $77,700-$78,000 band holds rather than getting retested.

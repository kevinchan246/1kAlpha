Weekly Recap: November 2–8, 2026

Week of November 2–8, 2026. The book closed the week at $984.09, down −$18.11 (−1.81%) from $1,002.20 at the previous close check (2026-11-01). No positions were bought or sold: all 12 check-ins ended in a hold. Against the $1,000.00 starting capital the simulation now stands at −$15.91 (−1.59%).

What mattered this week

A rally the triggers didn't see, then a crash none of them caught either

NAV opened the week near $1,004, climbed to a week-high $1,012.03 by Wednesday's close, then gave all of it back and more in a single Thursday session before settling into a quiet, flat weekend at $984.09 — the first time this book has closed below its $1,000 starting capital. Both of the week's two biggest moves came from catalysts this log had no trigger written for. Tuesday's election produced the "shock" JPMorgan had modeled — a full Democratic sweep of Congress, not the split outcome markets had priced — and the market's answer was a relief rally (the Dow up roughly 2.5-2.7%, the S&P 500 about 1.6%, the Nasdaq 1.8% at the open), not the roughly 0.5% drag forecast. Thursday reversed it entirely: a broad rotation out of AI and chip names, on fears that unified Democratic control plus state regulators would tighten oversight of AI buildout and data-center spending, took the Nasdaq down about 5.3% and the S&P 500 about 4.1% — by some counts its worst session since 2025. Twelve check-ins, zero trades, through both.

SPY's weight still hasn't been decided

The 35% concentration cap has been named every check for weeks, and this week it stopped drifting and just sat there. SPY reached roughly 35% of the book by Tuesday on appreciation alone, and it is still at almost exactly that level today (~34.9%) — through Wednesday's rally and Thursday's crash alike, because the rest of the book moved by similar proportions in both directions. Oil's intraday low near $93.50 on Tuesday was this trigger's first real piercing of the $95 floor it needs, but price closed back above $95 that same afternoon after a US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island, and the 10-year yield (still around 4.83-4.88%) never got near the 4.60% line the other leg needs. So the honest state heading into next week is the same one the log kept writing down without acting on: this weight arrived through price, not a decision, and a week that included the sharpest single-day drop in the book's life still wasn't enough to force the question.

NVDA fell further from the bar that would restore it

The position cut to half size three weeks ago needed two confirmed closes above $212-213 to come back; instead NVDA closed the week at $190.50, further from that level than it started. Thursday's AI-spending selloff did more damage here than the two-session bounce earlier in the week undid. The DOJ's Hugging Face inquiry remains open and unescalated, and the next scheduled catalyst is NVDA's November 17 earnings print.

Bitcoin absorbed a bad week without flinching

Roughly $1.28 billion in net spot-ETF outflows this month, a brief 11-week low in the mid-$70,000s, and reportedly over $1 billion in liquidations across the week — and BTC still never came within shouting distance of either line that would actually change the view: the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) or the short-term-holder cost basis (~$67,100). The position closed the week essentially flat, which is the least interesting and most correct outcome for a position sized to take exactly this kind of week without being re-sized by sentiment.

What would change the view

- NVDA — two confirmed closes above $212-213 restores full size; the November 17 earnings print is the next scheduled catalyst either way. - SPY — oil needs a confirmed close below $95 and the 10-year below 4.60%, together, with price back in the pre-trigger range; Tuesday's reversal moved further from that, not closer. Separately: the position has now spent a full week pinned near the 35% cap through a rally and a crash, and nobody has yet answered whether that weight is one this plan would choose today starting from cash. - Bitcoin — a confirmed close below the 200-day moving average (~$74,168) is the first real break to watch; sustained ETF outflows without a price recovery make that somewhat more likely, not less.

Still on the watchlist

ABNB — Watching · Blowout Earnings, Further Out of Target Zone · ≈$190 (hit a 52-week high after a blowout Q3 beat)
Airbnb reported Q3 results after Thursday's close and beat decisively: $3.16 EPS against a $2.84-2.85 consensus, revenue up 19% to $4.87 billion against a $4.75 billion estimate, gross booking value up 21%, Q4 guidance raised above consensus, and a new $12 billion buyback authorization on top of $0.7 billion already repurchased this quarter. Shares gapped up and have traded as high as the low $190s, touching a 52-week high — well above the $165-167 level this list noted at the last check, and further still from the $150-160 pullback zone that was always the entry condition here, not a story about the stock's quality. A result this clean removes the near-term case for a retest: there is nothing left to do here unless a fresh pullback, on its own terms, brings price back toward that zone. Chasing it at a 52-week high, on the strength of a print that already happened, would repeat exactly the sentiment-chasing mistake this plan has avoided elsewhere.

SPCX — Watching · Mission Success, Muted Reaction · ≈$149.80 (up modestly after Starship Flight 14's first successful orbital flight)
Starship Flight 14 lifted off from Starbase this morning at 8:49 ET and did what this list has been counting down to for two checks running: reached orbit for the first time and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, the mission's first revenue-generating payload. The Super Heavy booster didn't relight all its engines on its descent attempt, though it managed what SpaceX described as a soft splashdown — a real if partial blemish on an otherwise clean flight. The stock's reply was muted, up only about 0.75% to $149.80, still roughly 34% below its June IPO high and short of the $154.72 pre-unlock close from two weeks ago. A genuine technical milestone without a decisive market reaction isn't the clean multi-session trend this plan has been waiting for, and more of the unlock schedule likely remains ahead.
